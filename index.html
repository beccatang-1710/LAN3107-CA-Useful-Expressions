Part 2a

Making small talk — Holiday plans (Unit 2)

Responding techniques

1. Echo words (4ai–iii)

A: I went to Tokyo Disneyland during my annual leave.
B: Disneyland? That sounds fun!

2. Positive comments / compliments (4bi–iii)

Chris: They served Italian food and I had the best lasagna I've ever tasted.
Jack: Wow, I also love a good lasagna.

3. Ask follow-up questions & bring up new topics

  • Echo question (4c ii–v): Did you? Disney sounds exciting.
  • Wh- question (4c vi–xii): How do you like the rollercoasters and shows?
  • Yes/No question (4c xiii–xv): Did you try their restaurants?

Examples of talking about your upcoming holiday plan

I'm heading to beautiful Bali next month and I genuinely can't wait to unwind. I've booked a quiet villa with a private pool and plan to do absolutely nothing but read, sunbathe, and maybe take a refreshing surfing lesson.
I'm so excited for my highly anticipated holiday in vibrant Taiwan. I plan to eat lots of delicious street food at the bustling night markets and see the amazing, breathtaking views from the iconic Taipei 101. It will be my fantastic first time visiting, and I can't wait to explore.
I plan to go to Japan. I'm excited to explore the bustling streets of Tokyo, where I can visit iconic landmarks like the Tokyo Tower and the Meiji Shrine. I also want to visit Kyoto's peaceful temples and beautiful gardens. I can't wait to try delicious sushi and ramen, and see the amazing views of Mount Fuji.
I'm going to Australia with my family. I want to see the famous Sydney Opera House and visit the amazing Great Barrier Reef. I also hope to see koalas, kangaroos and other wildlife. I'm really excited for this big adventure.
My plan for the holiday is simple. I will stay home, sleep a lot, order my favorite foods, and watch good movies. I just want a peaceful and restful break.
Part 2b

Making small talk — Work projects (Unit 4)

Three stages — use the correct tenses

Tense Function Form
Present perfect To describe what has been completed recently Have/has + past participle verb — e.g., 'We have completed the product trial.'
Present continuous To describe what is happening now Am/is/are + present participle verb — e.g., 'Our marketing team is organising a promotional campaign for the health and wellness products.'
Future tense To describe what will happen Will + base form of verb — e.g., 'We'll launch the new line by the end of next month.'

Example (Task 3aii–ix):

Design an eco-friendly home decor line made from sustainable materials
 →
Task in progress

 →
Work to complete

We are currently creating a marketing plan to engage our target audience and promote our new eco-friendly home decor line. So far, we have designed an eco-friendly home decor line made from sustainable materials. Next, we will introduce our new eco-friendly home decor line with a launch event to showcase the features of the new product line.

Useful words & phrases

Currently, …… (present continuous)

Currently, the marketing team is launching social media contests.
I am happy to tell you that we are currently liaising a new advert plan with a renowned YouTuber.

So far, …… (present perfect)

So far, we have designed an eco-friendly home decor line made from sustainable materials.
Our team has already kicked off a trial of the new system.

Next, ……. (future)

Next, we will introduce our new eco-friendly home decor line.
If everything goes well as planned, we anticipate a rise in sales in all our outlets.

Giving details

Purpose — …… to … / To ……, ……

Next, we will introduce our new mobile application with a launch event to showcase the features and membership plans.
To encourage purchases, we will offer exclusive discounts and promotions to email subscribers and social media followers to retain existing customers and attract new ones.
To achieve this, we have partnered with experienced designers and manufacturers to create a collection that embodies our brand's values and aesthetic.

Details — …… including ……

We'll provide the influencers with detailed product information, including the features and benefits, to let them create content for posting on their own social media accounts.

Results — …… so that ……

We will conduct tests to ensure that the pages of the new product line function smoothly so that our customers can have a seamless shopping experience.
Part 3

Share your opinions on a new company policy (Unit 5)

Starting a conversation (about company facilities / policies / employee benefits)

Have you heard about…?
What do you think about…?
Did you hear about…?

Expressing opinions

I think / feel / believe / reckon / love…
In my view / opinion, …
On the one hand, … On the other hand, …

Stating causes & explaining effects

  • Causes: …because / since / as…
  • Effects: …so… / As a result, … / Therefore / Consequently, …

Sharing personal experiences

My position is that…
Personally speaking, …
My personal feeling is that…
My own experience is that…

Giving examples: For example / instance, …

Examples — Pet-friendly (Pet Day) policy

Positive:

Fantastic! I love this policy as I will be able to spend time with my dog at work. I feel that having pets in the office can definitely improve/enhance the atmosphere. It will be fun to see different pets, too. For example, some staff members may bring their cats or dogs, while others may bring more exotic pets, such as tortoises or parrots. I really believe that this pet-friendly policy is meaningful since having pets around can reduce the stress of many staff members and improve/enhance their work output.

Negative:

While I understand your view, I tend to think that it is quite inappropriate to have animals in the office because they can be very disturbing. It will be hard to concentrate with animals around. This may decrease productivity. In addition, I feel that no consideration has been given to health and safety issues. There are people who don't like animals or are allergic. This policy is unfair to them. In my opinion, we should focus on work in the office and keep our pets at home.

Neutral:

I have mixed feelings about Pet Day. On the one hand, having pets at work could be beneficial to some staff members and boost morale. On the other hand, the pets could be disruptive and noisy. It is a bit impractical to implement this policy every month. Perhaps we could consider a compromise, like running Pet Day twice a year. This may be more effective.
